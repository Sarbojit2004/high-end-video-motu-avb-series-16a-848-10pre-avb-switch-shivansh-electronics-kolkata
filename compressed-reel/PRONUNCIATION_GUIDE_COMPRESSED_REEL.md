# Pronunciation Guide — MOTU AVB Compressed Reel (88 s)

For the voice talent recording `public/vo/voiceover-compressed-reel.mp3` against
`VO_SCRIPT_COMPRESSED_REEL_MOTU_AVB_ECOSYSTEM.md`. Every term that appears in
that script is listed below in the order it's first spoken, with a plain-English
phonetic and a short note on the one or two places a reader is likely to guess
wrong.

**How to read the phonetics:** capitals mark the stressed syllable. No IPA —
these are meant to be read at a glance in the booth, not decoded.

---

## Brand & product names

| Term | Say it | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **MOTU** | MOH-too | One word, rhymes with "photo" minus the ph. Not spelled out as letters (M-O-T-U), and not "MOE-too." |
| **Mark of the Unicorn** | as written | The full name behind the MOTU initialism — spoken once, in the CTA. Plain English, no stress traps. |
| **Shivansh Electronics** | shih-VAHNSH | Stress on the second syllable, soft "sh" at both ends. Rhymes loosely with "Nirvana" cut short. |
| **CueMix Pro** | KYOO-mix pro | Two words run together as one in speech — "Cue" as in "queue," then "Mix." Not "COO-mix." |
| **AVB** | ay-vee-bee | Spelled out, three letters, no attempt to say it as a word. |
| **10pre** | TEN-pree | Say the number, then "pre" as in "prequel." Not "ten-P-R-E." |
| **16A** | SIX-teen AY | Number first, then the letter "A" on its own. Not "sixteen-uh." |
| **848** | EIGHT FOUR EIGHT | Read as three separate digits, not "eight hundred forty-eight." This is the house style across every reel in this pipeline — it reads faster and matches how the product name appears on the chassis silkscreen. |

## Technical terms

| Term | Say it | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **ESS Sabre32 Ultra** | E-S-S SAY-ber THIRTY-TWO UL-tra | "ESS" is spelled out. "Sabre" rhymes with "neighbor," not "saber" the sword (same sound, just flagging it). "32" is spoken as "thirty-two," not "three two." |
| **Thunderbolt 4** | THUN-der-bolt FOUR | Plain English compound word, number spoken as "four." |
| **USB4** | U-S-B FOUR | Letters spelled out, then "four" — not "U-S-B-four" run together as one sound. |
| **Milan** (certification) | mih-LAHN | The AVB networking standard, styled after the Italian city's pronunciation — **not** "MY-lan" (which is how the US city in Ohio/elsewhere is said). This one is easy to get wrong on a first cold read. |
| **gPTP** | jee-P-T-P | The lowercase "g" is still spoken as a letter ("jee"), then the rest spelled out. Doesn't appear in the spoken script directly but shows up on screen next to "Milan · IEEE 802.1" — flagged here in case a read includes on-screen labels. |
| **AVB Switch** | ay-vee-bee switch | Same "AVB" as above; "Switch" is the plain English word, capitalized because it's the product name, not a generic switch. |

## Money & GST

| Term | Say it | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Rs.** | "rupees" | Never read as "R-S" or "rupee sign" — always the full word, and always plural ("rupees"), even before a number. |
| **1,87,900** | "one lakh, eighty-seven thousand, nine hundred" | This is the Indian numbering system (lakh = 100,000), not the Western "one hundred eighty-seven thousand, nine hundred." The script already spells this out in words for exactly this reason — read the words on the page, not the digits. |
| **lakh** | lahk | Rhymes with "sock," not "lake." One syllable. |
| **52,990** | "fifty-two thousand, nine hundred and ninety" | No lakh needed at this size — read as ordinary English thousands, exactly as scripted. |
| **GST** | jee-ess-TEE | Three letters spelled out — Goods and Services Tax. Never expanded to the full phrase in this script; always just "G-S-T." |
| **MOP** | (not spoken) | Appears on screen as "Market Operating Price (MOP, incl. GST)" but the script never says "MOP" aloud — it says "Market Operating Price" in full where it's spoken, or is implied by context. If a future cut of the VO adds it, say "M-O-P," not "mop" the word. |

## Web & contact

| Term | Say it | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **www.shivanshelectronics.in** | "www dot shivansh electronics dot in" | Exactly as scripted — "www," then "dot," then the brand name run together as one word ("shivanshelectronics"), then "dot in." Do not add "double-u double-u double-u" (too slow for this reel's pace) or drop the "www" — the script's own phrasing is the fast, natural read that already fits the beat. |
